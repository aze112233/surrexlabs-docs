# Proposal lifecycle

A strong proposal makes a decision easier to evaluate and an approved mandate easier to execute.

## Stage 1: Signal

Share the problem and evidence early. The goal is to find whether the community considers the opportunity relevant before substantial work is spent on a formal proposal.

## Stage 2: Draft

Use a consistent structure:

1. **Summary** - the decision requested in plain language.
2. **Problem** - who experiences it and what evidence supports it.
3. **Proposed product** - the smallest credible solution.
4. **Strategic fit** - why SURREX should act.
5. **Scope and milestones** - what will and will not be delivered.
6. **Resources** - budget, people, infrastructure, and dependencies.
7. **Success metrics** - measurable adoption, delivery, or economic outcomes.
8. **Risks** - technical, market, legal, security, and governance concerns.
9. **Reporting** - update cadence and accountable owner.
10. **Conflicts** - proposer relationships or potential benefits.

For a venture entering the proposed build market, also identify the idea originator, proposed revenue model, expected operating costs, and how usage, retention, feedback, and revenue will be reported. These details support evaluation and future attribution; they do not establish eligibility for the proposed originator or backer allocations.

## Stage 3: Review

The community tests the draft. High-impact proposals may require specialist, security, legal, or treasury review before becoming eligible for a vote.

## Stage 4: Back and greenlight

The deck proposes that holders back ventures with $SRX and that higher conviction moves ventures higher in the execution queue. The published selection rules must define how backing leads to a greenlight decision and how the studio's capacity and required approvals are considered.

Where a formal governance vote is required, the final text should freeze before voting begins. Voters must be able to see exactly what is authorized, including the requested resources and success criteria. The deck does not specify a backing threshold, voting period, or automatic funding trigger.

## Stage 5: Execute

After selection and any required approvals, the mandate enters the public build pipeline. A proposal may define staged funding so later resources depend on accepted milestones. The studio scopes, builds, tests, deploys, and launches the product, with progress visible to the community.

## Stage 6: Close or renew

Launched work is evaluated through usage, retention, feedback, and revenue. A close-out or renewal report records what shipped, what was learned, and whether to expand, iterate, or stop. A new vote may be required for material expansion, additional budget, or long-term operation. Results inform the next venture cycle.

{% hint style="warning" %}
A passing vote is not a promise of investment return or a waiver of applicable law, security review, technical feasibility, or third-party platform rules.
{% endhint %}

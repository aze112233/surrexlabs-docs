# Proposal lifecycle

A strong proposal makes a decision easier to evaluate and an approved mandate easier to execute.

## Stage 1: Signal

Share the problem and evidence early. The goal is to find whether the community considers the opportunity relevant before substantial work is spent on a formal proposal.

## Stage 2: Draft

Use a consistent structure:

1. **Summary** - the decision requested in plain language.
2. **Problem** - who experiences it and what evidence supports it.
3. **Proposed product** - the smallest credible solution.
4. **Strategic fit** - why SURREX should act.
5. **Scope and milestones** - what will and will not be delivered.
6. **Resources** - budget, people, infrastructure, and dependencies.
7. **Success metrics** - measurable adoption, delivery, or economic outcomes.
8. **Risks** - technical, market, legal, security, and governance concerns.
9. **Reporting** - update cadence and accountable owner.
10. **Conflicts** - proposer relationships or potential benefits.

## Stage 3: Review

The community tests the draft. High-impact proposals may require specialist, security, legal, or treasury review before becoming eligible for a vote.

## Stage 4: Vote

The final text should freeze before voting begins. Voters must be able to see exactly what is authorized, including the requested resources and success criteria.

## Stage 5: Execute

After approval, the mandate enters the public build pipeline. A proposal may define staged funding so later resources depend on accepted milestones.

## Stage 6: Close or renew

Completed work receives a close-out report. A new vote may be required for material expansion, additional budget, or long-term operation.

{% hint style="warning" %}
A passing vote is not a promise of investment return or a waiver of applicable law, security review, technical feasibility, or third-party platform rules.
{% endhint %}
